02·B — Qur'anic Anchoring

Let the order name its source

The seal already is the cosmology of Qur'an 67:3 — but it cites it silently. So we do what classical Islamic seals and coins did: ring the mark with the very verse that describes it. The geometry is untouched; the Word becomes the outermost order, encircling life at the centre. No dome, crescent or arabesque — the relationship is made visible through inscription, not decoration.

Health in the driver's seat

Dr. Javed Jamil · 14 min read

For three centuries the modern world has placed economics at the centre of its order, and demanded that everything — including life itself — orbit the market. The result is measurable, and it is catastrophic.

The Dynamic Paradigm of Health proposes the inversion: a civilisation should optimise for health and life, and subordinate politics and economics to that single end. Health is not a static state to be restored, but a living process to be sustained.

The physician's task is no longer the patient alone, but the civilisation. He becomes the Doctor of the World.

Health Maximisation as the Optimisation Target of a Civilisation

Javed Jamil1
1 · Centre for Applied Islamics
ABSTRACT

We argue that prevailing economic paradigms systematically subordinate health to profit, and propose an inversion in which life and health become the optimisation target. Empirical estimates suggest 80M annual preventable deaths attributable to this misordering.

The modern order places economics at its centre and demands that all systems orbit the market. This paper formalises the Dynamic Paradigm of Health as an alternative ordering principle, in which clinical and civilisational health are treated as a single continuous process rather than discrete states.

Section 2 develops the measurement framework; Section 3 reports expenditure–outcome divergence across 41 economies.

Received 12 Mar · Accepted 04 Jun · doi:10.0000/jai.3.2.114
