#### Continuing with the debate:

#### DC Nath:

> We are proud to declare:

> · that, we, in India, that used to be called as Bharat, as stated in the Indian Constitution, are a multicultural, multilingual, multi-religious yet a homogeneous group of population;

![](https://javedjamilhome.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/26869-1oawsx4cioguiwmlstr1kzg.jpeg?w=736)  

#### Javed Jamil

**_In principle, we agree to the statement. Unfortunately though multicultural, multilingual, multi-religious parts are regularly compromised with. Leftist and other anti-religion forces try to negate the very importance of religion (something which religious leaders do not fully comprehend and confront with a united effort) and Hindutva forces try to suppress non-Hindu, cultures and languages. Some Muslim leaders too tend to react sentimentally rather than logically and legally. Urdu is being gradually sidelined despite its being a language of Indian origin, just because after Partition Pakistan chose to make it its official language. At present, our beloved country happens to be homogeneous neither politically nor socially and economically; this is despite the fact that impoverished masses do believe in all these values and this homogeneity is in jeopardy just because certain forces do not want homogeneity on account of their vested political and economic interests._**

#### DC Nath:

> · that the culture of India has all along shown tolerance, believed in synthesis and has been inclusive, composite as also syncretic. it has always permitted dissonance as a road to progress.

#### Javed Jamil:

_Muslims too have played an important role in this effort to preserve tolerant, positive and syncretic culture of India. But unfortunately again, the talk of tolerance is more theoretical than real. With the kind of violence against minorities, lower castes, Dalits and tribes we have been witnessing in increasing magnitude, our claim of being tolerant becomes hollow. On daily basis, provocative statements are made by leaders targeting one another. Instead of finding positives, we are trying to find, even manufacture weaknesses in others. Do recent killings of Pansare, Dhabolkar and Kalburgi, and above all the lynching of Akhlaq point to our tolerance? Have you forgotten Muzafafr Nagar riots, Mumbai riots and Gujarat riots that killed more than 3000 people? And the irony is that the forces that are most violent claim to be most peaceful. The Hindutva forces only remember Terrorists, which too only when the killers are allegedly Muslims. They forget that the terrorism caused by non-Muslim terrorists (Naxalites, Ulfa, Bodo, Maoists, Sikhs) have been responsible for more than 95 pc of terrorist related deaths. They also forget that the most dangerous terrorism of the recent world history that killed more than 1.5 lakh people in Sri Lanka was by Hindu Tamils. Partition saw more than a million deaths. So our claim of intolerance is not strong enough. The past history is also replete with incidents of huge intolerance. Still we feel pride that at least we, most of us if not all, have preserved the principle of tolerance as our faith._

#### DC Nath:

> · that India had never attacked any country;

#### Javed Jamil:

**_Individually I believe in Universalism (because the whole earth belongs to God, and not just one country) but Muslims as a community have always supported the national position in defence and foreign matters. Unfortunately though, all countries tend to claim they are the most peaceful nations, and still almost always act in accordance with their own interests rather than the interests of justice. This is true for India and Pakistan, all Muslim countries, all Western countries, China, Russia — in fact every single country on the face of the erath, in relative if not in absolute terms. People of all countries always try to glorify and defend their history, their culture and their positions in wars and confrontations. All are only relatively right or wrong, none absolutely. In countries like America, there are a large number of people who strongly condemn the foreign and defence policies of their own governments, but in countries like Indian Subcontinent, South Asia, hardly anyone takes positions against the national policies. Hopefully, in future it is universalism and not aggressive nationalism that prevails in the world. I strongly believe in utmost patriotism but I do not entirely agree with the kind of nationalism, which is being pursued in the current world._**

**_Also see the next answer to examine if India never attacked any other country. This question is illogical because “India” has continued to change in its dimensions over thousands of years. If you mean Post-Independent India, we have fought 4 wars during a small period of 70 years. Nationalistically speaking we are right in claiming that it is the other side which attacked us but the world historians will not always agree with our nationalistic sentiment. As proud Indians we all will always want to prove our case right._**

#### DC Nath:

> · that India’s expansion from Africa, Middle East to South East and Far East has all along been cultural, whose evidence comes up occasionally even now. One of the legendary Chinese scholar-diplomat and thought leaders, Hu Shin had exclaimed, “China was overwhelmed, baffled and overjoyed. She begged and borrowed from this munificent giver (India). China’s indebtedness to India can never be fully told.”

![](https://javedjamilhome.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/f0c45-1zwzrzkuc3qwx_f1hj2j5gg.jpeg?w=736)  

#### Javed Jamil:

**_Again this is a nationalistic rather than historical statement. Historically, you will have to first explain what India is. The present day India has continued to be ruled by numerous dynasties and rulers, all fighting each other to expand their empires. Naturally, Maurya, Ashoka, Mughals, all expanded their empires through invasion of nearby and distant areas. See the_** [**_link_**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_monarchs)**_._**

**_The link gives the names of hundreds of rulers and their areas and wars, which make clear that the rulers of different parts opf India were constantly on the warpath invading and conquering each other’s areas. Here are some of the most important rulers who expanded India:_**

· [Shishunaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shishunaga "Shishunaga") (412–344 BC), established the [Magadha Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magadha_Kingdom "Magadha Kingdom")

*   [Mahanandin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanandin "Mahanandin") (until 424 BC), his empire was inherited by his illegitimate son Mahapadma Nanda

[Mahapadma Nanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahapadma_Nanda "Mahapadma Nanda") (from 424 BC), illegitimate son of [Mahanandin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanandin "Mahanandin"), founded the [Nanda Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_dynasty "Nanda dynasty") after inheriting

· Dhananand (_Agrammes_, _Xandrammes_) (until 321 BC), lost his empire to [Chandragupta Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya "Chandragupta Maurya") after being defeated by him.

· Karvinatha Nand (Illegitimate son of Mahapadna Nanda)

The nine kings were contemporary and brothers, called the Nava Nandas (nine Nandas), ruling at different parts of Magadh (present [Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar "Bihar") state of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India")) headed by the strongest ruler of all the then 14 Janpads (Kingdoms) of India; Dhananand.

· [**Chandragupta** Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya "Chandragupta Maurya") ([Chandragupta Maurya the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya_the_Great "Chandragupta Maurya the Great")) ([Sandrakottos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandrakottos "Sandrakottos")) (322–298 BC), founded the [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Empire "Mauryan Empire")after defeating both the [Nanda Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_dynasty "Nanda dynasty") and the [Macedonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Macedonians "Ancient Macedonians") [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire "Seleucid Empire"), claimed descent from the [Shakya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakya "Shakya")dynasty, the first emperor of India who unified most of northern India.

[**Ashoka** Vardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka_the_Great "Ashoka the Great") ([Ashoka the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka_the_Great "Ashoka the Great")) (273–232 BC), considered the greatest [ancient Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_India "History of India") emperor, first emperor to unify India (after conquering most of [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan")), adopted [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), granted [animal rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_rights "Animal rights") and promoted [non-violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-violence "Non-violence")

· [Satadhanvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satadhanvan "Satadhanvan") (195–187 BC), the [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan_Empire "Mauryan Empire") had shrunk by the time of his reign

*   [Samudra Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudragupta "Samudragupta") ([Samudragupta the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudragupta_the_Great "Samudragupta the Great")) (335–370). Reputed to have never lost a battle in his whole life.
*   [Jatavarman Sundara Pandyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatavarman_Sundara_Pandyan "Jatavarman Sundara Pandyan") (1251–1268), revived Pandyan glory, considered one of the greatest conquerors of[Southern India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_India "Southern India")
*   Vira Pandya (1308–1311), son of Maravarman Kulasekaran, fought with his brother of Sundara Pandya over the throne,[Madurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurai "Madurai") was conquered by the [Khilji dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khilji_dynasty "Khilji dynasty")
*   [Muhammad bin Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_bin_Qasim "Muhammad bin Qasim") (711–715), an [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab "Arab") general, conquered [Sindh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh "Sindh"), [Balochistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balochistan_%28region%29 "Balochistan (region)") and southern [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_Pakistan "Punjab, Pakistan") and ruled these lands on behalf of the Ummayyid Caliph, [Al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Walid_ibn_Abd_al-Malik "Al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik")
*   [Rajendra Chola I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Chola_I "Rajendra Chola I") (1012–1044), expanded the [Chola Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_Empire "Chola Empire") overseas to [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia")
*   [Alauddin Khilji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alauddin_Khilji "Alauddin Khilji") (1296–1316), considered the greatest of the Delhi Sultans, unified India and defeated a number of invading [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire") armies
*   [**Tipu Sultan**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipu_Sultan "Tipu Sultan") (_Tiger of Mysore_) (1782–1799), son of Hyder Ali, considered the greatest ruler of Mysore, assumed the novel style Badhshah Bahadur of Khudadad (thus claiming the paramountcy of India instead of the Mughal ‘mere’ Badhshah), fought the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company "British East India Company"), [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha "Maratha") and [Nizams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizam "Nizam") of [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad_State "Hyderabad State") in the 3 [Anglo-Mysore Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Mysore_Wars "Anglo-Mysore Wars") (where iron [rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket "Rocket")) were first used, allied to the French, and lost everything
*   [Muhiuddin Muhammad Aurangzeb Alamgir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb "Aurangzeb") (1658–1707), expanded the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") to its greatest extent, ruling over most of [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan").

​**_You will know that the whole history of the region is full of wars, offensive as well as defensive, North, South, East and Western portions of India being ruled at the same time both by different independent rulers as well as by single rulers like Chandragupta Maurya, Ashoka, Akbar and Aurangzeb who incidentally had the biggest Indian empire. Recently, we returned several parts of land to Bangladesh. These were returned obviously because we captured them some time. The truth is that the history of India has been one of the bloodiest in the world with uncountable wars between rival kings. The other parts of the world too have witnessed similar histories. Mankind has unfortunately always loved to confront one another because instead of following God alone, they have followed their own whims. Like most if not all other world histories, cultural expansion in India followed armed expansion and not the other way round. India became Buddhist after Ashoka conquered it. Later most reconverted but the new Hindu culture assimilated the elements of Buddhism like Ahimsa including vegetarianism. After Muslim rule, the culture witnessed another round of change with new languages, dresses, food habits, festivals, music, poetry and customs emerging. British Empire brought another round of change, and fortunately or unfortunately, our present system and culture is more westernised than Hindu or Islamic._**
