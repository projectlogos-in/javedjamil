The **Human Development Index** (**HDI**) is the most known indicator to measure the status of development of the countries of the world. The **Human Development Index** (**HDI**) is a composite statistic used to rank countries by level of “human development” and separate [developed](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country "Developed country") (high development), [developing](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country") (middle development), and [underdeveloped](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underdeveloped "Underdeveloped") (low development) countries.

Starting with the 2010 report the HDI combines three dimensions:

*   **A long and healthy life: Life expectancy at birth**
*   **Access to knowledge: Mean years of schooling and  
     Expected years of schooling**
*   **A decent standard of living: GNI per capita (PPP  
     US$)**

Like all the major international criteria, **HDI is a criteria developed by West-dominated organisations to promote certain development patterns, which obviously are not only market-friendly but are also designed to develop an image of development for the Western countries that are predominnatly functioning as free-market economies based on the commercialisation of all human needs including their susceptibilities**.. Not only the three criteria have been chosen in a calculated manner to paint a positive image of Western countries neglecting what is negative there, each one of them has been designed to project a certain picture. **No surprise then that the Human Development Index (HDI) can in fact be regarded as the measurement of westernisation of different nations;** the higher a country ranks in the list of HDI the more Westernised it is. It can however be seen on closer scrutiny that not only the three major criteria fail to capture the true picture of the goodness of human life but each of them tries to suppress certain criteria of fundamental importance.

1.  **The biological life of human beings starts immediately after conception and not after birth, and that from medical point of view, human life has two stages: prental and postnatal. The prenatal life is in fact so important that several specialities of medicine have been designed to safegaurd the prenatal lives.**

#### **Other Indices also Faulty**

The two other indices are also faulty and are only a measure of the acceptance of Western models by different countries. For example, **the per capita income the way it is calculated does only tell about the total incomesof the countries divided by their population, and fails to take into account the fact that in most countries the economic disparity is huge.** This disparity is certainly more in the countries that have adopted Western models of capitalistic, market friendly models of economics**. In several countries, 90–95 pc of the wealth is in the hands of 5–10 pc population.** The result is that the per capita income of a country cannot be taken as a true index of the prosperity of the majority of the people of a country. Instead, **if the per capita income of the fifty per cent of the population, having lowest income is calculated, this will give a better idea of the comfort level of the masses and hence the “development” level of a particular country.**

Even the criterion used to determine the “access of knowledge” is faulty and again gives a picture of **accessability to the Western pattern of education**.**Knowledge cannot be restricted to “modern” education, which hardly tells about the morality and do’s and don’ts of true peace but only tells the success route to enjoyment in life through employment in corporate sector. The knowledge of Scriptures including Quran-literacy is no criterion in the eyesof the present champions of “Human Development”.**

It is necessary that “development” level must be measures on the basis of comprehensive criteria. The criteria must be atleast the following

**1\. Total Life Expectancy (from conception)**

**2\. Access to any type of knowledge;**

**3\. Per capita income of the 50 percent of the people with lowest incomes**

**4\. Level of Sexurity: an index comprising murder, suicide and rape statistics;**

**5\. Family peace: index must include rate of divorce/separation, children born out of wedlock and children living in single parent families;**

Other criteria including the alcohol related problems, accident related morbidities, etc can also be included.
