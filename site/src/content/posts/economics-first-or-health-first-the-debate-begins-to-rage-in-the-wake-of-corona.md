> **_Unfortunately, while in principle, the “modern” world boasts of an extraordinary concern for human life and health, in truth the other interests rule the roost. It will hardly be an overstatement that health has in effect become a perpetual captive of economics. The international system of governance is neither keen nor has been doing what it needs to do to attain the highest standards of health. The tragedy is that they are so powerful that the mankind cannot hope to challenge them unless a global revolution emerges. But the truth remains that health needs to be liberated without delay and positioned in the driver’s seat with the brakes and the accelerator in its safe possession._**

“Economics First or Health First?” is the name of my latest book, which was released in August last year. Interestingly, this has become the title of the debate, which the Corona pandemic has unfolded in the world. The debate has specifically begun to rage in the United States. The US is in a state of dilemma because it is in the midst of the preparations of the Presidential elections, and the present President hopes to return back to the White House. **He is in a state of dilemma: should he save the lives of the people or save the economy from reaching a moribund state? On the one side, there are the market forces, which have been the major supporters of President Trump; on the other are the people and it is their votes that ultimately matter.** Till now, he has been inclined more towards saving the economy rather than the lives, and has desisted from clamping total lockdown like many other countries. The result is that already more than 5000 people have died and now the Government is talking of facing a situation where the numbers of the dead may be between 100000 and 240000. Faced with the criticism in many circles, in last couple of days, he has started saying that for him lives matter more than economics. **In the midst of the debate, the ultimate reality is that, whatever the strategy – lives first or economy first—it is ultimately the economy which will fix the strategy. The economists are busy weighing the cost of both positions. On the one hand, there is a situation where too many Americans can die ultimately crippling the economy on a long term basis. On the other hand, harsh measures are taken to save the lives with immediate loss to economy. It is the dilemma between short term and long term effects on economy, which will be the final decider.** Already, experts are arguing that Corona only kills the old; so for the sake of the old, why should the young be kept away from work? The loss of 1 pc of population can be neutralized by not allowing a certain percentage of abortions. Already, people are complaining that their right to abort is being denied through the Corona threat. Then, the industry is already busy calculating the likely benefits in the healthcare industry, the industry of the protective gears and ventilators.

Ideally speaking, we need a system in place in the world, which decreases mortality and morbidity, reduces the global burden of diseases, increases true life expectancy (with conception being the starting point of life) and decreases the burden of the cost of maintaining health. The current world systems based on modern economic ideologies do just the opposite by exposing people to the dangerous social practices, and try to minimise the damage through artificial means of treatment and vaccinations. Speaking in medical terms, this policy of massive industrialisation of aetiologies followed by industrialisation of management has become the greatest burden on mankind. In the rivalry between the economics and the health, the economics is the clear winner. 

When HIV/AIDS started turning into a disaster in the 1990s, the forces of economics made sure that the WHO does not make any recommendations that could affect the huge sex industry. Despite the unequivocal facts that the disease spread due to promiscuity, prostitution and homosexuality, there was no attempt whatsoever to launch a campaign against these practices or to target the sex industry. Instead, the whole emphasis was on the use of condoms. As soon as the anti-HIV drugs emerged, the AIDS prevention campaigns became less and less vocal and frequent. The international agencies are not interested in preventing the diseases for which treatment or vaccines are available by pointing to the original practices that lead to these. Even in the case of Corona, the furore the world is witnessing now will suddenly come to naught as soon as the vaccine or treatment is found. The underlying statement is clear: diseases and healthcare are both parts of the market and health will continue to remain secondary to economics. 

I will like to conclude the article with an excerpt from the book that I mentioned at the beginning of the article:

> _Health relates to the very existence of human beings. Survival and the quality of survival are undoubtedly the most essential aspects of living, as individuals as well as collectively as families, communities and societies. Unfortunately, while in principle, the “modern” world boasts of an extraordinary concern for human life and health, in truth the other interests rule the roost. It will hardly be an overstatement that health has in effect become a perpetual captive of economics. The international system of governance is neither keen nor has been doing what it needs to do to attain the highest standards of health. The tragedy is that they are so powerful that the mankind cannot hope to challenge them unless a global revolution emerges. But the truth remains that health needs to be liberated without delay and positioned in the driver’s seat with the brakes and the accelerator in its safe possession._
