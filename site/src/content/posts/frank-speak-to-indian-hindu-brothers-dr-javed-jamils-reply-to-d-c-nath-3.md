#### **that India had Muslim Presidents, Chief Justices of Supreme Court, Union Home Minister, Ambassadors (any numbers), the first Education Minister of independent India, even a Director of Intelligence Bureau. Let us not forget the legendary and the bravest of the brave Indian soldier Hamid Ali, who fought off the Pakistani invaders in Kashmir.**

_In the Name of God, Kind-Merciful, the Only One worthy of Worship, every other entity, animate or inanimate, great or holy, being His humble servant_

B**efore moving further, I will like to make a brief comment on the current wave of communal polarisation in the country.**

When privatisation was sought to be introduced in the late 1980s, a simultaneous communal agenda was set into motion with the launching of the Ayodhya Movement. The country forgot what was boiling at the economic front and the political and social organisations, including the Leftists, busied themselves with combating the monster of communalism. This continued till privatisation got fully entrenched. The situation was then allowed to settle down. Now, with the installation of Modi Government, the second round of corporatisation has not only begun but is in full swing, and every government scheme — good as well as bad — is being tuned in to suit the interests of big business.

Economic forces, including the corporate and the government supporting them, know the likely effects of the move on the public mind. They fear that the forces that are more concerned about the common people can embark upon a nationwide campaign against corporatisation. It is therefore necessary that the public mind is kept busy somewhere else. What else can be the best option other than using the time-tested card of communalism? With hatred engulfing the nation, the social forces and political parties like Congress, Janata Parivar and communists will again be busy countering communalisation. Economic forces can play their card without attracting public attention. Once the second round is finished, the situation will be eased again for some years in wait for a suitable time for the next round. Media, which is nothing but an extension of Corporatocracy, is clearly a party to the whole plan. Every day it finds one event or one statement and then the whole day and night keeps the viewers glued to heated debates. There is never any debate on the current state and solutions of economic disparity, the role of various forces in poverty, inflation and corruption, the rising crime rate, family disintegration, fast eroding moral and social values, devastating effects of alcohol, drugs, gambling, unhealthy sexual practices, nudity, party culture, negative effects of the kind of “freedom of choice” being propagated by the forces of economics, the numerous health issues and hundreds of other social vices (including female foeticide, dowry, domestic violence, rapes, prostitution, exploitation of the poor, etc).

**Now coming to your letter:**

#### D C Nath

> **· that India had Muslim Presidents, Chief Justices of Supreme Court, Union Home Minister, Ambassadors (any numbers), the first Education Minister of independent India, even a Director of Intelligence Bureau. Let us not forget the legendary and the bravest of the brave Indian soldier Hamid Ali, who fought off the Pakistani invaders in Kashmir.**

#### Javed Jamil

_Yes, Muslims have played an important role in almost all fields including Sciences & Technology, Defence System, arts, culture and politics. And Indian Tourism largely owes its success to Taj Mahal and other buildings built by Muslim kings. But thanks to the various policies and circumstances, they have not got share in positions anywhere near their percentage in the population. India has never had any Muslim Prime Minister, any Finance Minister, only one Home Minister, just two Chief Ministers (outside J & K), very few Parliamentarians, both in Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, and very few administrators and bureaucrats. For this situation, government policies, legal provisions as well as Muslims themselves are responsible._

_“Constitution Amendment Act or CAA recognises that governance can no longer remain solely the prerogative of governments. Government needs to become a collaborative effort of the government and non-government sector, a fusion of public and private initiatives of citizens particularly in governance. As such, the creation of suitable structures and process is vital to the success of democratic decentralisation. This process must include Muslims who should be allowed to become a partner in governance especially in the areas related to their healthy and prosperous living._

_Muslims have much lesser role in governance because they have just 2.5 percent of the administrative and executive services. This is drastically lower than the 15 percent required for representative influence on the policies of the government. This is true not only in IAS, IFS, IPS and allied services but also in state level administrative jobs. The judiciary also has much lower presence of Muslims. The same is true of the electronic and print media. The presence of Muslim Ministers, MPs, MLAs — are all much below the numbers they deserve in accordance with their population. They are therefore effectively in no position to influence the direction of the nation or the policies of the government. Moreover, the psyche with which they are imbued has a hidden fear of being dubbed “communal” or “Islamist” in case they feel inclined to say what they think. More often than not they are reluctant to take decisions that may appear to be in favour of their community or a position that can be closer to Islamic ideology. Additionally, their educational background and their long conditioning during training and service tilts their way of thinking in the direction the others around them have and what they read and hear in the media and public and private gatherings._

_If government, parliament, judiciary, military, executive and administration, all the important wings of governance do not have adequate representation of Muslims, how can Muslims get their share in the governance? This has to be reversed without wasting any time. The government, the political parties and the community — all will have to play their role. The government must ensure that the number of ministers in the cabinet as well as in Parliament and Assemblies must not be less than the required share. It must find ways to increase the share of Muslims in administrative services through reservation and through special drives to raise the level of education among Muslims and by attracting more and more Muslim students towards civil service exams._

#### D C Nath

> **· that, despite being butchered, pillaged, looted and forced to being converted, India never capitulated completely. Its resurgence capability has been astounding.**

#### Javed Jamil

_This will be contested by a large number of historians. Books after books have been written. I want to discuss the present, not the past. What applies to India applies to almost all the countries. Rulers belonging to all communities including Hindus have used force to subdue areas. There is no bigger example than Ashoka who rose from a small area and then captured all the surrounding areas, and converted almost all the Hindu population to Buddhism. India capitulated totally to Ashoka, almost all converting, and if Muslim rulers had used the kind of brutality and destruction that Ashoka did, India would have perhaps capitulated wholly to them also. However, for most part, they cared for the religious freedom of the people, and those who converted to Islam mainly did so because they recognised that_

· _Islam was true extension of the original messages contained in previous religions mandated by God,_

· _that it had the most purified concept of oneness of God,_

· _that it has a complete system related to all aspects of life,_

· _that it calls for equality — religious, social and economic (without discrimination on the basis of sex, caste, creed, race, nationality), justice and brotherhood; its God is the Lord of the Universe, not of any single community, generation, nation, time or planet;_

· _that its permissions and prohibitions are totally based on the benefits or harms to mankind._

_These teachings they received mostly from the saints, preachers and sufis, not from the rulers. It is to be noted that when almost all Hindus converted to Buddhism after the conquest of Ashoka, they reconverted to Hinduism after his departure. This clearly shows that they had converted only out of fear. In case of Muslims, they have continued to adhere to Islam even after more than two centuries of the Muslim rule. In fact, many are still converting to Islam. This is true for almost the entire world and the maximum rates of conversion are in European, American and Australian continents. In fact, in India, the rate of conversion could have been much higher if family and social pressures on individuals were not huge, and if some communities had not feared the loss of certain privileges in case they choose to convert. If the people all over the world are thronging to Islam, it is obviously because they think that it is the final version of God’s religion, the best meeting ground of all previous God-guided religions and not the departure from them), and the best system to confront the corruption of modernity._

_I will advise to better discuss history with historians and they will tell you much more about how things have happened in the past._

_But I will like to assert here that no single country on the face of the earth including ours can be proud of its whole history which has many positives and no less number of negatives. And the irony is that heroes for some often happen to be villains for others, and vice versa._
