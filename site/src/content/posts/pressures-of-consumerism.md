#### By blaming consumerism, they want to shift the blame for the current socioeconomic problems of the world on the common people. They seem to suggest that, if they are selling consumer items, and are earning high profits out of it, it is simply because the people are becoming increasingly inclined to use these items. Had there been no demand, they argue, how they could market them. They ignore the fact that the demands are not always natural; more often, they are artificially created. It must also be clear that the demands are not always genuine; many of them are based on covetousness, and are in total disregard of their impact on the surroundings. All demands, therefore, need not be met with. For example, if there is demand for destructive weapons, would they be made available in the open market; if there is demand for poison for the sake of killing others, would it be made available to whoever seeks it? For most of the supplies made by the market forces, no demand was in fact made by the people. The truth is that the majority of people do not even have any prior knowledge of what would be supplied to them in the coming future. The economic fundamentalists study the weaknesses of human beings, and then exploit them in creating demands. And after the creation of demands for a certain item, they attempt to monopolise its market by popularising a specific brand.

#### Pressures of consumerism

The present economic system has travelled a long way to establish a hold of the economic fundamentalists in the world. It can be summed up that this system is mainly based on the creation of demands through whatever means possible – disinformation, high-pitched advertising of the positive aspects of the products, reconditioning of tastes and institutionalization and commercialisation of human weaknesses. Terms like “consumerism” and “capitalism”, too, are in fact deliberate creations of the economic fundamentalists. By blaming consumerism, they want to shift the blame for the current socioeconomic problems of the world on the common people. They seem to suggest that, if they are selling consumer items, and are earning high profits out of it, it is simply because the people are becoming increasingly inclined to use these items. Had there been no demand, they argue, how they could market them. They ignore the fact that the demands are not always natural; more often, they are artificially created. It must also be clear that the demands are not always genuine; many of them are based on covetousness, and are in total disregard of their impact on the surroundings. All demands, therefore, need not be met with. For example, if there is demand for destructive weapons, would they be made available in the open market; if there is demand for poison for the sake of killing others, would it be made available to whoever seeks it? For most of the supplies made by the market forces, no demand was, in fact, made by the people. The truth is that the majority of people do not even have any prior knowledge of what would be supplied to them in the coming future. The economic fundamentalists study the weaknesses of human beings, and then exploit them in creating demands. And after the creation of demands for a certain item, they attempt to monopolise its market by popularising a specific brand.

Similarly, to blame capitalism is also erroneous. It also provides the economic fundamentalists with a tool which they use with tremendous effect. They can say that they are earning profits, even if somewhat on the higher side than is expected from them, because they invest the capital. Without capital, they assert, production is not possible and therefore they deserve profit for that. It is now the right time to tell them that if capital is important for production labour is no less essential, Furthermore, if capital is a “previously done labour” and is therefore, entitled for profit, why is the ‘current labour’ enti­tled only for wages and not for profit. Still more important is the fact that even the capital they boast of, is only partially theirs; a large portion of it also comes from the people, either through banks or stock exchanges. They may, of course, be entitled to some additional gains for their entrepreneurship, but the importance of professionals, the skilled and unskilled labourers, and the money of the people cannot be denied; for, without them, entrepreneurship would not have any value. Justice, therefore, demands equitable distribution of profits among all those who contribute in production.

It can be said that the development of modern economic theory has not been without resistance from certain quarters. But what is unfortunate is that even those economists, who be­lieved in maintaining equilibrium between the individual and society, could not see that economics was only a part of the whole social system. They showed concern hither and thither for the health, family system (in fact, many believed in the disintegration of the family system) and social order, but they could not streamline their views into a well-knit, elaborate system of peaceful existence of all human beings.

#### Commercialisation of human susceptibilities

While consumerism as a whole accentuates economic disparity, as it entices less-moneyed people to spend more than what they must, commercialisation of human weaknesses is particularly dangerous. Alcohol, drugs, smoking and gambling are addictions that more often than not result in extreme poverty. The whole family suffers. The person addicted suffers with adverse effects on his health, recurrently leading to loss of his working hours with decreasing incomes, these addictions consume all his income. The effects on health of the entire family, owing to less consumption of food and inability to meet the expenses related to health trap the family in an unending cycle. These habits are also the major causes of suicides, crimes and accidents.

#### Market-sponsored feminism

Market sponsored feminism has also accentuated disparity. The experts of “growth” knew that the concentration of income in some families is more likely to help their growth model rather than the distribution among larger number of families. They have therefore advanced a concept of gender equality, which helps their cause. This is especially true for a country like India where unemployment is high. There are families where both husbands and wives are working, and there are many men who are not getting employment. Furthermore, with women coming out of the homes, the fortunes of many businesses shine including cosmetics, fashion, hotelling and sex.

#### Faulty distribution (of Population, Resources)

The distribution has gone awry in every respect. There is huge concentration of population in big cities. The villages are getting sparsely populated compared to cities. The resources too are getting concentrated in a few pockets. Of course, the power and wealth are there in a few hands. All these lead to huge economic disparity.

#### Fast Urbanisation with neglect of Rural Development

The cruel Trickle Effect is being pursued in infrastructural development as well, with the level of development fast decreasing from metropolitan cities to other big cities, from big to smaller cities, from cities to blocks and from blocks to villages.

The neglect of rural development means impoverishment of people living in villages. There is hardly any electricity in the villages with long cuts, roads are rusty, health facilities are minimal, educational institutions are concentrated only in urban areas, the cost of the land in villages is low, and the villagers are in no position to engage in marketing of the agricultural products themselves. The big business takes advantage of the land produce in a big way while farmers earn a little.

The governmental policies are to be discussed in detail particularly how these policies are adjusted to suit the needs of the corporate.
