Some Clarifications:

1.  Noon and noonghunna are often the cause of confusion. Where noon is sounded as noonghunna, it does not carry any wazn like in jhaaN-knaa (212), maaN-gna(212),, phaN-saa (12), jhaaN-saa (22), etc. In words like raNg, bhaNg, saNg, despite the sound of noonghunna, it is counted as noon, these words will have wazn of 21.
2.  In words like pyaar, pyaas, the wazn will be same as paar (21) and paas. The same is true for words like kh(w)aahish(22) and kh(w)aab (21), where waw is largely silent. However, old poets sometimes used them as piyaar (121), piyaas(121), KHuwaab (121) and KHuwaahish (122).
3.  The words like meri and teri can be used as mey-ree (22), me-ree (12). Mey-ri (21), me-ri (11). Abhee (12) can also be used as abhi (11), same for kabhi
4.  chhoti ha, yaa and alif can be suppressed if used at the end of the word (mainly in verbs and the words like bhee, hee) etc but ain and bari Ha never. Na (noon chhoti ha) is always used as having 1 wazn, use of it as 2 is considered bad. Yeh (2) and ke (kaaf chhoti ha) can be used as 1 also.
5.  Many words can be used in different wazn. For example, Allah (221) can be used as All-lah (22) and Al-la (21)
6.  ja-gah (12) can be used as jag-ha (21) also but waj-ha (21) cannot be use as wa-jah (12). Similarly shah-r (21) cannot be used as sha-har (12). Tar-ha (21) can also be used as ta-rah (12). Waz-n (21) cannot be used as wa-zan.
7.  The last words with jazm appearing in the end of a misra are not counted. For example, if jaam is appearing at the end of the misra, its m will not be counted, and the wazn will remain 2 and not 21, its normal wazn.

Now, from next lesson, we will study bahrs. For the beginning, I am leaving one misra here of a very popular Mahdi Hassan Ghazal with its mathematical wazn. The readers must try to match the other misras, which will be found exactly following the same rhythm:

raf-ta raf-taa woh me-ree has-tee ka saa-maaN ho ga-ey  
2 1 2 2 2 1 2 2 2 1 2 2 2 1 2

Please note that first rafta is used as 21 and the second raftaa as 22. The readers can very well see following other misras where alif, waav, yaa and ha are suppressed and where not.
