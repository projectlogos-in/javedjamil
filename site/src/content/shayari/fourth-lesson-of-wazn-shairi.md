One clarification before going forward:

If a word is followed by another word which begins with alif, the two words can be pronounced in a different way changing the original wazn of the words. For example,

aaKHir is dard kee dawaa kyaa hai

In this shair the wazn of aaKHir is22 and is is also 2 but it will be read as aa-khi-ris which would make the wazn as 212, which is the correct wazn of these words in this specific bahr.

Here are the mathematical equivalents of the well know units of bahoor:

Fe-lun (fa ain laam, nun, with saakin on ain) 22  
Fa-i-lun (zabar on fa and zer on ain) 112  
Fa-oo-lun 122  
Faa-i-lun (212)  
Faa-i-laa-tun (2122)  
Ma-faa-i-lun (1212)  
Ma-faa-ee-lun (1222)  
Mus-taf-i-lun (2212)  
Muf-ta-i-lun (2112)  
Mu-ta-faa-i-lun (11212)  
Maf-oo-lun (222)  
Maf-oo-l (221)  
Fa-i-laa-tun (1122)

Another popular bahr: 2122121222

(The underlined and bold 2 can be divided into 11)

The above GHazal is also in the same bahr. So is the case with:

Ib-ne mar-yam hu-aa ka-rey ko-ee  
2 1 2 2 1 2 1 2 2 2

In the following misra, the 2 in the nderlined position is divided into 11:  
Ko-i taa-zaa ha-waa cha-lee hai a-bhee  
2 1 2 2 1 2 1 2 1 1 2

(This is the same bahr to which the ghazal with the controversial shair “Utar aaye suraab aankhoN meiN belongs.)

To remember the patterns, another idea has come to me. I will use Qa as denoting 1 and dam as 2 and then we can remember the bahr more easily. For example the above bahr kan be weitten as

Dam qadam dam qadam qadam dam dam
